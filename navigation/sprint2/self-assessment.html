Design notes

1
DesignOne scale, every point labeled

The current form mixes two scales (Explore→Create and Beginning→Model). A Likert survey normally uses one agreement scale. Use the toggle to compare both.

2
DesignStatements written as "I…"

An agreement scale only reads naturally against a statement. The 4 categories and their meaning stay the same.

3
Design · Coding2 Pros / 1 Grow replace the reflection box

Three separate fields instead of one. Each needs 20–500 characters, enforced in the browser and again on the server.

4
DesignOptional "Related to" tag

Links each Pro or Grow to one of the 4 categories, so teachers can see why a student rated themselves that way.

5
DesignProgress chips

Show what's left before Submit works, instead of a single error after the click.

6
SecurityLocked after grading

Prevents changing a self-assessment after seeing the grade. Edits before grading are kept in history.

7
Security · CodingNo identity in the payload

The request has no user id or score. The server takes the student from the login session and shows every text field as plain text in the teacher view.

Questions for the project manager

The answers change what gets built, so these come before coding. They're grouped by the three work areas in the directive.

Design

  1. Who is "2 pros, 1 grow" about?

    Is it the student reflecting on their own work, or a peer giving feedback on someone else's submission? Peer feedback needs reviewer assignment and very different screens.

  2. Agreement scale or rubric levels?

    "Likert-style" usually means Strongly disagree → Strongly agree. Rubric levels (Explore → Create) can be compared directly with teacher scores. Which matters more?

  3. Are the 4 categories fixed?

    Should every assignment use Technical Excellence, Communication, Work Habits and AI Orchestration, or can a course or teacher change them?

Coding

  1. Who sees the results, and do they affect grades?

    Teacher only, the student's own history, peers, or a class summary? Is it purely reflective, or part of the score?

  2. Required on every submission?

    Required each time, once per assignment, or optional? What happens on a resubmission: a new survey, or edit the old one?

  3. What reports do you want from the data?

    For example, self-rating vs. teacher grade, trends across a sprint, or common "grow" themes. This decides how the data is stored.

Security / tamper

  1. Which tampering worries you most?

    Changing answers after grading, submitting as another student, editing the grade through the API, or low-effort answers like all 5s and copy-pasted text?

  2. Should answers be editable?

    Locked at submit, editable until graded, or always editable with a visible change history?

  3. Do we need to check for AI-written or copied reflections?

    If so, a warning to the teacher is enough, or should the submission be blocked?
